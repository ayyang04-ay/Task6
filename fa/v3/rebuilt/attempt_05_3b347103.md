# 1. Recommendation

**Keep AX-1177, but retire its viral-LRTD positioning.** In short: keep the molecule, drop the indication label.

**Confidence in the two halves of the case differs sharply:**

- **High confidence that the molecule works.** It does what an anti-IL-33 antibody should. It almost completely neutralises the endothelial activation driven by reduced IL-33. The effect is isotype-controlled, and there is no detectable off-target transcriptional effect.
- **Low confidence that the target matters in patients.** The only human evidence is indirect. Soluble ST2 (sST2), the circulating decoy form of the IL-33 receptor, is #2 of 1,094 serum analytes for progression to ICU, invasive ventilation or death.

**The gap that matters:** our data do not show that IL-33 is elevated, released, or causally involved in viral LRTD. IL-33 itself was never measured in a patient.

**What to do:**

- **Re-scope** the asset to the hypothesis the data support: *IL-33-driven endothelial injury in hospitalised severe lower-respiratory infection, enriched by sST2*.
- **Freeze** clinical-enabling spend.
- **Fund a short gated package** (Section 5) with pre-agreed kill criteria. The first gate is re-analysis of data we already own.
- **Drop** the asset if the gates fail.

| Question | Answer from our data |
|---|---|
| Does AX-1177 neutralise IL-33 signalling? | **Yes.** IL-33 changes 4,026 genes in HUVEC at 6 h; AX-1177 reverses induced genes by a median of 95%, even when added with IL-33 and no pre-incubation |
| Is the IL-33 axis linked to severe disease in patients? | **Indirectly.** sST2 ranks #2 of 1,094 for ICU/IMV/death (OR 3.02 per log unit, FDR 0.0075; adjusted for age, sex and NEWS2). But about 190 proteins are associated, so this is not specific |
| Is IL-33 itself elevated or released in patients? | **Unknown.** IL-33 is absent from the delivered serum data. Tissue IL-33 shows no COVID- or pneumonia-specific change |
| Is "viral LRTD" the right indication label? | **No.** The data do not separate viral from non-viral pneumonia. IL-33 also drives an endothelial antiviral programme that AX-1177 blocks |
| What the data point at | A host-response endotype: **endothelial injury (sST2-high) in hospitalised severe lower-respiratory infection at risk of progression**. Not a different disease area |

# 2. The case

## 2.1 What our data establish: the case for keeping

**1. The molecule works, and works cleanly** (Fig 1, Fig 2).

- **The IL-33 response.** In HUVEC, reduced IL-33 (30 ng/mL) changes 4,026 genes at 6 h and 1,831 at 24 h (FDR < 0.05, genes with valid statistics).
- **The blockade.** AX-1177 (Compound_47) was added *at the same time* as IL-33, with no pre-incubation. Against the isotype-matched control it reverses IL-33-induced genes (more than 2-fold induced) by a median of **0.95 at 6 h** and **0.73 at 24 h**.
- **The NF-κB core.** NFKBIA, TNFAIP3, NFKB1/2, RELB, TRAF1, NFKBIZ, RIPK2 and SDC4 are reversed by 97–105%, with |t| of 25–44 in both directions.
- **The isotype control is inert at 6 h.** NIP228 shifts IL-33 responses by a median of only 0.04 log2.
- **A second endothelial bed responds too.** In glomerular microvascular endothelium (HGMEC), AX-1177 changes 1,456 genes at 6 h in the presence of IL-33 (vs isotype). These changes mirror the HUVEC IL-33 programme (ρ = −0.53) at about half the magnitude.
- **No detectable off-target effect.** Antibody alone, against isotype or PBS, gave 13–73 "hits" per contrast across four cell types. These are all near-floor, low-abundance noise, with no reproducible overlap between timepoints or cell lines (Table S3).

**2. The response it blocks looks like severe-pneumonia endothelial injury** (Fig 2). The IL-33 programme in HUVEC at 6 h has several arms:

- **Neutrophil recruitment and myelopoiesis:** GM-CSF +10.3 log2, G-CSF +8.3, CXCL2/3/5/6 +5 to +9.4 (CXCL1 and CXCL8 similar but with broken statistics).
- **Adhesion:** E-selectin and VCAM-1 (+11.2 and +8.7 log2, statistics broken) and ICAM-1 (+6.0, FDR 0.01).
- **Pro-coagulant:** tissue factor (F3) +5.6, t = 16.9.
- **Complement:** C3, CFB.

**3. The IL-33 axis is among the strongest serum signals of progression** (Fig 3). The cohort is 100 hospitalised, pre-vaccination COVID-19 patients, 44 of whom reached ICU, invasive ventilation or death within 30 days.

- **sST2** is #2 of 1,094 assays: OR 3.02 (1.83–5.01) per log unit, FDR 0.0075, adjusted for age, sex and NEWS2.
- **Endothelial-injury proteins** are also associated: HGF (#1), PTX3, VEGF-A, vWF, ANGPT2, sFLT1 and tissue factor.
- **Several IL-33-inducible, AX-1177-reversible products are outcome-associated:** IL-6 (on 4 independent panels, OR 2.07–2.31, FDR ≤ 0.019), CCL20, OPG, CSF-1, MCP-3, IL-18R1 and tissue factor.

**4. Tissue is compatible with endothelial IL-33 taking part in lung inflammation** (Fig 4).

- IL-33 is present at moderate-to-high levels in lung endothelium and basal epithelium in all 31 donors.
- Endothelial IL-33 is *lower* where neutrophils are higher: age-adjusted partial Spearman −0.43, p = 0.015.
- That fits the release of a pre-stored nuclear alarmin. This is an interpretation; semi-quantitative IHC cannot prove it.

**5. The remaining uncertainty is narrow, and much of it can be resolved with what we already hold.**

- The count matrices and fitted model are in the programme archive.
- Patient-level Olink data exist behind the summary table.
- Lung RNA is linked for 22 donors, and the ID series suggests all 31 donors have it.

## 2.2 What our data do not establish: why not "continue as is"

**1. The target was never measured in a patient.** IL33 is missing from the delivered serum data.

- It is absent together with 14 other Inflammation-panel assays that are usually undetectable in serum (TSLP, IL-4, IL-5, IL-13, IL-1α, IL-2 and others).
- The per-group below-detection fractions reported for delivered assays never exceed 9%. This is my reading of the composite_endpoint_0/1 columns, whose values are multiples of 1/56 and 1/44. It is consistent with a detectability filter having removed IL-33.

**2. The sST2 signal is prognostic, not specific.**

- About 190 of 1,063 unique proteins (18%) are associated with the endpoint, and 98% of those go in the same direction.
- Most of the top 40 are not IL-33 endothelial outputs. They reflect:
  - myeloid activation: CLEC5A, OSM, PRTN3;
  - intracellular proteins released from dying cells: NPM1, CALR, GSTP1, PRDX1/6;
  - tissue repair: HGF.
- sST2 is also a decoy receptor. A high level could mean more IL-33 activity or less free IL-33. We cannot tell which without measuring IL-33.

**3. The IL-33 endothelial programme is not over-represented among severity proteins.**

- 61 measured proteins have genes that IL-33 induces and AX-1177 reverses. Of these, 25% are outcome-associated, against 19% of all other proteins (Fisher OR 1.38, p = 0.31).
- The most strongly IL-33-driven secreted products do not track outcome at all: G-CSF (p = 0.23), CXCL6 (p = 0.95), CXCL5 (p = 0.52), VCAM-1 (p = 0.43) and CXCL1 (p = 0.28–0.46).
- Across 902 proteins there is only a weak positive trend between how strongly IL-33 induces a protein and how strongly it associates with outcome (ρ = 0.13). That is what generic NF-κB inflammation would produce.

**4. Tissue gives no COVID- or virus-specific IL-33 signal.**

- Donor-level IL-33 scores do not differ between COVID+ pneumonia (16 donors), COVID− pneumonia (10) and non-pneumonia lungs (5), in any compartment (p 0.11–0.65).
- Endothelial IL-33 trends *lower* in COVID+, not higher.
- Granulocyte markers separate pneumonia from non-pneumonia (p 0.004–0.01) but not COVID+ from COVID− pneumonia (all p > 0.3).

**5. The in-vitro package is not yet a clinical package.**

- HUVEC and HGMEC each come from a single donor, so the replicates are technical, not biological.
- There is one antibody concentration, at about a 41-fold molar excess over IL-33, so there is no potency or dose–response information.
- Only reduced IL-33 was tested. The annex says AX-1177 also binds oxidised IL-33, which is its potential differentiator, but that is untested.
- No epithelial cell was ever challenged with IL-33. A549 and MCF7 received antibody only, and MCF7 is a breast line.
- The endothelial model is umbilical vein, not lung microvasculature.
- There is no virus in any experiment.

**6. A mechanistic liability specific to a viral indication** (Fig 2).

- IL-33 induces an endothelial interferon/antiviral programme with valid statistics: MX1/2, OASL, ISG20, IFIT1–3, RSAD2 (viperin), DDX58 (RIG-I), IFIH1 (MDA5) and CXCL10.
- This programme persists to 24 h.
- AX-1177 blocks it in both endothelial types.
- Dosing during active viral replication could therefore blunt antiviral defence. This has not been tested.

**7. The statistics for the genes that bridge to the clinic are broken in the delivered tables** (Fig 5).

- 238 genes (0.9%) have implausible standard errors, up to about 37,000 log2 units.
- They include IL1RL1 (the ST2 gene), PTX3, SELE, VCAM1 and CXCL8.
- Their fold changes replicate across contrasts built from disjoint libraries. For example, SELE is +11.2 with IL-33 and −11.0 with AX-1177. The estimates are probably real, but no inferential claim can be made for these genes until the model is re-fit from counts.

## 2.3 Why "keep, re-scoped and gated" rather than "drop now"

- **The negatives are mostly absence of evidence.** They come from an assay that could not see IL-33 and an IHC score that sits at ceiling (53% of endothelial blocks score the maximum). The positives — pharmacology and sST2 — are real.
- **The decision-critical questions are few and well defined:**
  - Is IL-33 released in patients who progress?
  - Does sST2 add anything beyond generic inflammation markers?
  - Does the effect hold in lung microvascular cells, across donors, and for oxidised IL-33?
  - Does blockade impair antiviral defence?
- **Gate 0 needs no new samples.**
- **Neither decision should rest on the broken statistics for ST2, PTX3, E-selectin, VCAM-1 or IL-8.** Re-fitting them is Gate 0.

## 2.4 Anticipated challenges, and the answers

| Challenge | Answer grounded in our data |
|---|---|
| "Any anti-IL-33 would give the same in-vitro result. Why keep *ours*?" | Correct: the in-vitro data validate function, not differentiation. Our potential differentiator, binding of oxidised IL-33, has never been tested. Gate 1 tests it, and a negative result is a kill criterion. |
| "Isn't sST2 just a severity marker?" | Possibly; it is one of about 190 associated proteins. That is why it is proposed only as a *prognostic enrichment* marker. Gate 0/2 must show it adds beyond IL-6/HGF and NEWS2, and that IL-33 itself is elevated. |
| "Tissue shows no IL-33 increase in COVID. Doesn't that kill it?" | Nuclear IL-33 is near ceiling, scored by a single reader, and block-to-block variance within a donor is as large as variance between donors. A released alarmin would *lower* staining, and endothelial IL-33 is indeed lower where neutrophils are higher. The tissue neither supports nor refutes the hypothesis; a quantitative release assay is needed. |
| "Why not keep the viral-LRTD framing?" | (i) The serum data are SARS-CoV-2 only, pre-vaccination. (ii) Tissue pathology is shared between COVID+ and COVID− pneumonia. (iii) AX-1177 blocks an IL-33-induced endothelial antiviral programme, which is a liability during viral replication. |
| "Is the RNA-seq reliable?" | The core conclusions rest on about 26,000 genes with valid statistics (e.g. NFKBIA t = 44). The 238 broken genes are flagged, and none of our inferential claims use them. |
| "Why not COPD or asthma, where IL-33 biology is strongest?" | Our data do not support it. IL-33 does not differ by COPD or asthma history (p ≥ 0.2). Serum shows no type-2 signal. No airway-disease model was run. It would be a new programme, not a repurposing justified by this package. |

## 2.5 If the committee drops it: what we are dropping and why

We would be dropping an anti-IL-33 IgG1 that, in our hands, almost completely neutralises reduced IL-33 in endothelial cells without off-target transcriptional effects. We would drop it because our data do not show that IL-33 is measurable, elevated, or specifically linked to outcome in patients with viral LRTD:

- the only human signal (sST2) is prognostic but non-specific;
- tissue shows no disease-specific IL-33 change;
- the mechanism we did characterise, endothelial activation with an interferon/antiviral arm, is a liability when the disease is an active viral infection;
- the differentiating claim (binding oxidised IL-33) was never tested.

# 3. Which indication our data point at

**The data point at a host-response endotype, not a pathogen category and not a different disease area.**

- **Mechanism: endothelium, not epithelium and not type-2 biology.**
  - Every IL-33 response in the package is endothelial.
  - Epithelial lines were never stimulated.
  - Type-2 markers are null in serum: IL-5 OR 1.00 (p = 0.997); eotaxin-1 OR 0.98–1.08; CCL24 OR 0.92.
  - Tissue eosinophil markers follow pneumonia in general, not COVID.
- **Population and stage: hospitalised patients at risk of progression.**
  - This is the population and endpoint where sST2 and endothelial-injury markers are prognostic.
  - It is defined at enrolment, and the associations hold after adjustment for NEWS2.
- **The pathogen boundary is not something our data define.**
  - Tissue findings are shared between COVID+ and COVID− pneumonia.
  - The serum data are COVID-only.
  - Extension beyond SARS-CoV-2 is therefore the *first hypothesis to test*, not a conclusion.
- **Timing: the host-response phase, not early viral replication.** This follows from the blocked antiviral programme.

**Proposed working indication:** *prevention of progression to ICU, invasive ventilation or death in hospitalised patients with severe lower-respiratory infection and biomarker evidence of endothelial injury (high sST2).*

This re-scopes the indication within acute lung injury; it does not repurpose the asset. Other indications were considered and are not supported (Section 6.1).

# 4. Biomarkers, sampling timepoint, and which markers can go into patients

**Sampling timepoint.**

- **In vitro: 6 h after IL-33 is the primary readout.** Reasons:
  - the IL-33 response is largest at 6 h (4,026 vs 1,831 genes at 24 h);
  - blockade is most complete at 6 h (median 0.95 vs 0.73);
  - the HGMEC blockade signal collapses by 24 h (1,456 to 269 genes);
  - the isotype arm drifts about 15% at 24 h.
- **In vitro: 24 h only as a secondary readout**, for the interferon/complement arm and antiviral-safety questions.
- **In patients: enrolment, before dosing, for sST2.** This is the only timepoint at which our data show an outcome association.
- **In patients: PD sampling early after dose** (for example 24 h and 72 h) as well as baseline. This timing is inferred from the in-vitro kinetics and must be confirmed.
- **Matrix: EDTA plasma, not serum.** Clotting releases platelet proteins such as CXCL5 and VEGF-A.

# 5. Next steps: what the studies must establish (gated, with kill criteria)

**Gate 0: re-analysis of data we already own (no new samples)**

- **RNA-seq:**
  - re-fit from the archived counts, per cell line or with checked weights;
  - confirm library identity;
  - deliver the missing contrasts (HGMEC IL-33 vs isotype; HUVEC isotype vs vehicle).
  - **Pass:** induction and reversal of IL1RL1, SELE, VCAM1, CXCL8 and PTX3 reach significance at 6 h, and HGMEC shows a quantifiable, reversible IL-33 response.
- **Patient-level Olink:**
  - report IL-33 detection by outcome;
  - standardise ORs per SD;
  - test whether sST2 adds beyond NEWS2 plus IL-6 and HGF.
  - **Pass:** sST2 remains independently associated.
- **Lung RNA:**
  - re-link the 9 unlinked RNA IDs;
  - score the HUVEC 6 h IL-33 signature (NF-κB core plus neutrophil/myelopoietic module) in post-mortem RNA;
  - re-score IL-33 IHC digitally, with two blinded readers.
  - **Pass:** the signature tracks pneumonia or neutrophils, or tracks loss of endothelial IL-33.

**Gate 1: in-vitro pharmacology a clinical package needs**

- **Cells:** primary lung microvascular endothelium and airway basal epithelium, at least 3 donors each. Basal epithelium is the IL-33-positive compartment in our tissue.
- **Stimuli:** IL-33red *and* IL-33ox.
- **Design:**
  - AX-1177 dose–response (IC50/IC90), with and without pre-incubation;
  - readouts at 1–6 h (primary) and 24 h;
  - TNF/IL-1β controls to define IL-33-specific markers;
  - viral co-culture to test antiviral impact.
- **Kill or deprioritise if:**
  - there is no activity against IL-33ox (no differentiation);
  - blockade is weak in lung microvascular cells;
  - AX-1177 increases viral replication (which excludes active-infection settings).

**Gate 2: human target validation**

- **IL-33 measurement:** form-specific, ultrasensitive IL-33 in plasma and BAL from hospitalised lower-respiratory infection (COVID, other viral, and bacterial) versus controls.
- **sST2 replication:** an independent, multi-centre, contemporary cohort with EDTA plasma and recorded symptom-onset timing, replicating sST2 with a pre-specified cut-off and showing incremental value over NEWS2 and generic inflammation markers.
- **Kill if:**
  - IL-33 (free or total) is not elevated in the lung compartment of progressors;
  - sST2 fails to replicate.

**Gate 3 (only if Gates 0–2 pass): first clinical study**

- **Population:** sST2-enriched hospitalised lower-respiratory infection, with aetiology set by Gate 2.
- **Dosing:** in the host-response phase.
- **PD:** pre-dose, 24 h and 72 h, using the panel above.
- **Target engagement:** total IL-33.
- **Safety:** viral-load kinetics and bacterial superinfection.

# 6. What I considered and ruled out

## 6.1 Indications

| Considered | Verdict | Why (our data) |
|---|---|---|
| Viral LRTD as currently framed | **Retire the label** | COVID-only serum; no IL-33 measurement; pathogen status not discriminating in tissue; blockade of an IL-33 antiviral programme |
| COVID-19 as a standalone indication | Not supported | No COVID-specific IL-33 or inflammatory signal in tissue; only pre-vaccination serum; no reason in our data to restrict to SARS-CoV-2 |
| COPD / asthma | Not supported | IL-33 does not differ by COPD or asthma history (p 0.2–0.9; n = 11 and 6); no type-2 serum signal; no airway model |
| Type-2 / eosinophilic disease | Ruled out | IL-5, eotaxin-1 and CCL24 null; tissue eosinophil markers are pneumonia-generic |
| Glomerular / renal endothelial disease (HGMEC responds) | Not supported | HGMEC response about half of HUVEC, gone by 24 h, cannot be sized (no vehicle arm); kidney markers only modestly associated (KIM-1 OR 1.66, p = 0.03; LCN2 OR 2.86, p = 0.003) in a respiratory cohort |
| Heart failure / cardiovascular (sST2 is an HF marker) | Not supported | No cardiac phenotype in our data; NT-proBNP barely associated (OR 1.20–1.24, p 0.05–0.17); the sST2 signal here sits within a broad inflammatory signature |
| Oncology (A549/MCF7 in package) | Ruled out | Lines never IL-33-stimulated; antibody alone had no reproducible effect |

## 6.2 Biomarkers

These were ruled out, or restricted to a narrower role:

- **Serum IL-33:** absent from the data.
- **Tissue IL-33 IHC:** at ceiling, single reader, no discrimination.
- **Type-2 and eosinophil markers:** null associations.
- **HGF, the #1 serum hit:** not IL-33-inducible in endothelium, so not a mechanism marker.
- **Intracellular "leakage" proteins (NPM1, CALR, GSTP1, PRDX):** reflect cell death and are not mechanism-specific.
- **VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 in serum:** not outcome-associated.
- **G-CSF, CXCL6 and CXCL5:** restricted to in-vitro PD.

# 7. Data-integrity notes for the committee (full log: Table S9)

- **mmc5:** the sheets are mislabelled duplicates.
- **Broken statistics:** 238 genes have implausible standard errors; the ST2, PTX3, SELE, VCAM1 and CXCL8 genes are among them.
- **Missing design elements:** no HGMEC vehicle arm or IL-33 contrast; no epithelial IL-33 arm; single-donor endothelial cells.
- **mmc2:** IL-33 absent; cross-panel duplicates counted separately in the FDR; serum matrix.
- **mmc3:** the IL-33 columns are *hidden* in the workbook. Other problems:
  - a formula stored as text;
  - 'N/A'/'ND' entries and trailing spaces;
  - an n+0.5 score coding that exceeds the stated scale;
  - one merged cell spanning two donors;
  - clinical notes on the second block for two donors, contrary to the annex;
  - 1–8 blocks per donor.
- **None of these changes the core pharmacology conclusion.** Several change what can be claimed about the ST2/PTX3 bridge and about tissue differences.

# 8. Methods (brief)

**Transcriptomics (26 contrasts × 26,210 genes).**

- Internal consistency was checked: P against t (implied df 75–85, consistent with robust eBayes), BH recomputation (exact), and AveExpr constancy.
- The implied SE (|logFC/t|) is bimodal. Genes with SE > 2 in any contrast were excluded from inference.
- Reversal was computed as −(AX-1177+IL-33 vs isotype+IL-33)/(IL-33 vs vehicle).

**Proteomics.**

- Olink IDs were mapped to genes and cross-panel duplicates collapsed (mean z, BH across 1,063 unique proteins).
- The link to the in-vitro data was tested with Fisher's exact and Mann–Whitney tests, and Spearman correlation.

**Tissue.**

- Donor-level means of block scores were used, with "n+" read as n.
- Comparisons used Kruskal–Wallis and Mann–Whitney tests, plus age-adjusted rank partial correlation.
